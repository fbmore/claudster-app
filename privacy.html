Privacy Policy

Last updated November 27, 2026

Claudster runs on your Mac and, if you use it, on your iPhone or iPad, which talks only to your own Mac. It has no account and no analytics. Away from home the iPhone or iPad reaches your Mac through Claudster's relay, end-to-end encrypted, so the relay can't read what passes through it. Nothing it handles is sent to us, ever. The one record kept about you is a Claudster Pro purchase, if you make one, held by RevenueCat under an anonymous identifier.

In short
  • We (FRANCESCO BERTOCCI, LLC) collect no telemetry, crash reports, usage statistics or update checks, and nothing from your sessions.
  • If you buy Claudster Pro in the iPhone or iPad app, RevenueCat keeps the purchase record under a random identifier, so Pro works and can be restored. No name, email or payment details.
  • Everything Claudster reads and writes stays on your Mac, in your own project folders, and on your iPhone or iPad if you use the app.
  • The dashboard is served by your Mac to your own devices. Other devices need your private link to read it.

What Claudster reads

To show what each Claude Code or Codex session is doing, Claudster reads files that are already on your Mac:

  • The status files its helper writes in each project, at .claude/status.json.
  • Claude Code's local session transcripts in ~/.claude/projects/, for the recap Claude Code writes and for images in the session (ones you pasted, and screenshots a tool returned), so a card can show them.
  • Claude Code's local image cache in ~/.claude/image-cache/.
  • The names and one-line descriptions of your slash commands and skills, to suggest them when you type / in a reply box: your own in ~/.claude/commands/ and ~/.claude/skills/, the project's in its .claude/ folder, installed plugins' (listed in ~/.claude/plugins/installed_plugins.json), and Codex's prompts in ~/.codex/prompts/. It reads nothing else from these files.
  • Files in your project folders that describe how a project looks (app icons, favicons, asset catalogs, CSS colors and web manifests), to give each card its icon and color.
  • The list of running processes and their working folders, to find which terminal runs which session.

What Claudster stores, and where

  • Settings, including your dashboard's access key, in ~/Library/Application Support/Claudster/.
  • Per-project status, in each project's .claude/ folder:
    • status.json: the session's state, a short headline and summary, the beginning of your latest prompt (up to 200 characters), Claude's latest reply (up to 600 characters), the command or file awaiting your approval, the branch, and terminal details (terminal app, window identifiers, process ID and your Mac's network name).
    • events.jsonl: a short history, at most 200 entries.
    • queue.json: tasks you queue from the dashboard.
    • screenshots/: screenshots attached to the card, at most 40 per project.
    Claudster asks Git to ignore these files through your global Git ignore file, so they are not committed unless you add them yourself.
  • Session images copied out of transcripts, at most 8 per session, in ~/Library/Caches/Claudster/.
  • Claude Code settings. Installing adds Claudster's hooks to ~/.claude/settings.json (backing it up first) and a skill to ~/.claude/skills/project-status/. If you also install for Codex (Settings › Claude Code › Codex), it adds the same hooks to ~/.codex/hooks.json (or the folder set by CODEX_HOME) and the skill to ~/.agents/skills/project-status/. Your other settings are left as they are.
  • Optional extras, only if you turn them on: a custom icon file inside each project folder, and titles and colors on Terminal tabs.

The dashboard and your network

Claudster serves its dashboard from your Mac over your local network, so you can open it on your phone. It is plain HTTP on your network, not the internet, and it is protected by a private access key:

  • Another device can only read or change anything with the link that carries your key (the phone link or QR code in the menu). Treat that link like a password.
  • The dashboard only answers requests addressed to your Mac, which protects it against web pages that try to reach it through your browser.
  • On a network you don't trust, such as public Wi-Fi, turn off Allow access from other devices on this network in Settings › General. The dashboard then answers only this Mac.

The iPhone and iPad app

The Claudster app for iPhone and iPad shows the same board as the dashboard and lets you send tasks. It connects only to your own Mac, using the same private access key: directly over plain HTTP on your network when the phone is on the same Wi-Fi, and through Claudster's relay when it isn't.

The relay, away from home. Your Mac keeps a connection open to Claudster's relay, which runs on Cloudflare Workers. When your iPhone or iPad isn't on the Mac's network, it connects to the same relay, and the relay passes data between them. Everything between the phone and the Mac is encrypted end to end (AES-GCM) with a fresh key for each connection, derived from the private access key, which never leaves your devices. The relay can't read or change what passes through it. It sees only a random identifier for your Mac, the anonymous RevenueCat identifier of your iPhone or iPad (to ask RevenueCat whether it has Claudster Pro), when connections open and close, and how much data passes. It stores nothing except a hash of your Mac's relay secret, and keeps no logs of content. Turn it off in the Mac app's Settings › Reach this Mac away from home; the phone then works only on the same Wi-Fi.

Claudster Pro purchases. Reaching your Mac away from home is Claudster Pro, bought in the app through Apple. Apple processes the payment; we never see your payment details. The app uses RevenueCat to check and restore purchases: RevenueCat receives a random identifier made by the app (not your name, email or Apple Account), the purchase receipt from Apple, and basic device information such as the iOS version and region. It is linked to nothing else about you and is not used for tracking or advertising. See RevenueCat's privacy policy. Without a purchase, the app only asks RevenueCat which plans are available.

Finding your Mac through your Keychain. So your own iPhones and iPads can connect without scanning a QR code, even away from home, the Mac app saves one item in your Keychain, which syncs to your devices through iCloud Keychain: your Mac's name, its network address and port, its private access key and its relay identifier. iCloud Keychain is end-to-end encrypted by Apple: only devices signed in to your Apple Account can read it, not Apple and not us, and only Claudster's own apps can open this item. We never receive it. Turn it off in the Mac app's Settings › Share this Mac with my iPhones through Keychain, which deletes the item from all your devices.

  • What it keeps on the phone: the access key, in the device's Keychain on this device only (the phone itself never syncs it; see Keychain above); your Mac's network address and relay identifier; only if you choose Remember after unlocking your Mac from the phone, your Mac's login password, in the Keychain on this device only, readable only after Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, and typed only on that Mac's lock screen; and your settings, such as alerts, haptics, text size and which sessions you follow. Session details (projects, questions, recaps, screenshots) are shown while the app is open and are not saved.
  • What it sends, and only to your Mac: the tasks you type or dictate, photos you attach or paste, what you share to Claudster from other apps with the Share button (images, files, links and text, sent only when you tap Send and only to the session you pick), and changes you make to the board, such as tile order and project looks.
  • Camera, only to scan the QR code that pairs the app with your Mac. No photo is taken or kept.
  • Microphone and Speech Recognition, only while you dictate a task. Apple's speech recognition turns your speech into text, on the device where it can and otherwise on Apple's servers, under Apple's privacy policy. Claudster keeps no audio; the text goes to your Mac like a typed task.
  • Photos: you pick photos with the system picker, so the app sees only the ones you choose.
  • Local Network, to find and reach your Mac.
  • Face ID, only to read a Mac password you chose to remember, right before it's typed on your Mac's lock screen.
  • Notifications, if you turn alerts on: you get an alert when a session needs you, with the project name and its question, and the Live Activity shows how many sessions are waiting or working. Your Mac sends these through Claudster's push service, which runs on Cloudflare Workers and passes them to Apple's push service. The alert text is encrypted on your Mac with a key only your Mac and your iPhone or iPad share, and decrypted on the phone; the push service and Apple see only the encrypted text, your phone's push token and, for the Live Activity, the counts. The push service stores nothing.

Deleting the iPhone or iPad app removes its settings. iOS can keep an app's Keychain items after the app is deleted, so to remove the access key and any saved Mac password, use Forget Saved Mac Passwords and Disconnect in the app's Settings first.

Permissions it asks for

  • Automation (Terminal, iTerm or System Events), to bring a session's terminal tab forward, to type the fixed line "Process the queued Claudster items for this project." when you choose Send now, and to label Terminal tabs if you turn that on. It never types anything else.
  • Notifications, to tell you when a session starts waiting on you or hits an error. The notification shows the project name and its question.
  • Local Network, to serve the dashboard to your phone.
  • Microphone, only when you dictate a task from the dashboard on your Mac (macOS 26 or later), or record part of your screen with a microphone chosen in the recording bar (off unless you pick one). For dictation the microphone is on only while the listening panel is open; speech is turned into text on your Mac by macOS's own speech recognition, and the audio is not saved and not sent anywhere. The text goes to the session the same way a typed task does. For a screen recording the microphone is on only while recording: your voice is kept in the video's sound track, and what you said is also written out, on your Mac, into a notes file with the times you clicked. Both are attached to the task you recorded them for and go only to that project on your Mac, like any other attachment. The first time, macOS may download its speech model for your language from Apple.
  • Microphone in a web browser, only if you open the dashboard in a browser on your Mac and dictate there. The browser asks for the microphone and turns speech into text with its own speech recognition, under the browser maker's privacy policy: Safari uses Apple's, and Chrome sends the audio to Google. Claudster receives only the text. In the dashboard page on a phone, use your keyboard's dictation; the page itself does not use the phone's microphone. The iPhone and iPad app has its own dictation, described below.
  • Accessibility, for two things. If you use Tile on sessions that share a Terminal window: Terminal has no other way to move a tab into its own window or merge it back, so Claudster presses Move Tab to New Window and Merge All Windows in Terminal's Window menu. And if you turn on Let the iPhone app type and move the pointer: the keys you type and the pointer moves you make in the iPhone or iPad app are passed to your Mac, including your password on the lock screen when you unlock it from the phone. Claudster does not read, log or keep what is typed. It does not read or press anything else.
  • Screen Recording: Claudster asks for it only when you watch a session's Terminal window, or a project's site preview, live in the iPhone or iPad app. It captures only that window, sends the picture to your phone (through the encrypted relay when you're away), and keeps nothing. Your terminal app may also be asked, when a Claude Code session uses Claudster's helper to screenshot an app window for its card.

What we do not collect

  • The app has no analytics, advertising or tracking identifiers.
  • No crash reports or diagnostics sent anywhere.
  • No accounts, names, email addresses or payment details. Apple handles payments for Claudster Pro.
  • No copy of your prompts, code, transcripts or screenshots leaves your devices because of Claudster.

Other services

  • Claude Code is made by Anthropic and has its own terms and privacy policy. Claudster only reads the files Claude Code keeps on your Mac and does not change what Claude Code sends to Anthropic.
  • Codex is made by OpenAI and has its own terms and privacy policy. Claudster only adds its hooks and skill and reads what they report; it does not change what Codex sends to OpenAI.
  • RevenueCat checks and restores Claudster Pro purchases in the iPhone and iPad app, as described above. RevenueCat's privacy policy applies to that record.
  • GitHub hosts this website and the downloads. GitHub's privacy statement applies to visiting it.
  • This website counts visits with Foliàire Analytics: pages viewed, how far down a page you scroll and how long you stay. It sets no cookies, keeps a random session identifier in your browser's local storage, collects no personal data and honours Do Not Track. The app itself sends nothing.
  • When a Claude Code session asks Claudster's helper to screenshot a web page, the helper opens it in a temporary, private copy of Chrome on your Mac, which is deleted afterwards.

Removing your data

See how to uninstall Claudster completely. Removing the hooks in Settings › Claude Code (for Claude Code and for Codex) stops all new status files, and deleting the folders listed above removes everything Claudster stored.

Children's privacy

Claudster is a tool for software developers and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone.
